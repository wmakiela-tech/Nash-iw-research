LEARNER HYPOTHESIS PRESERVED:
Dark matter may be hard to detect because it forms few or unusual relations with visible matter; extreme versions might place the dark carrier in a different sector, geometry or relational structure, coupled to us mainly gravitationally.
REC9 CORE:
Do not replace this with the weaker slogan 'many models give same observations'. The learner's relation-sparsity question is legitimate, but relation COUNT alone is not the right invariant.
Use three typed layers:
RELATION GRAPH / COUPLING TYPE
-> SOURCE-NATIVE PRODUCTION AND ADMISSION

A. MATURE HOST / PRIOR ART
Strong overlap already exists with weakly coupled particle DM, hidden sectors, purely gravitational production, self-interacting DM, bimetric/massive-gravity sectors, braneworld/D-brane models, and modified/emergent-gravity or hybrid models.
Critical distinctions:
WEAK OR ZERO SM COUPLING != ABSENCE FROM SPACETIME.
HIDDEN SECTOR usually means hidden gauge/portal structure, not another spacetime.
GRAVITY IS ALREADY A CROSS-SECTOR RELATION.
Different branes may be distinct localization sectors while remaining in one higher-dimensional bulk.
BIMETRIC != automatically two unrelated universes.
H4 emergent/relational geometry is not one theory class.
Checked mature anchors include hidden-sector thermal relics, purely gravitational production with isocurvature constraints, D-brane dark sectors in a higher-dimensional bulk, and GW170817-based constraints on specific braneworld geometries.
B. GENUINE REMAINDER AFTER PRIOR-ART SUBTRACTION

A model-specific scientific remainder survives:
Can a dark carrier with a strictly sparser or differently typed legal cross-sector relation graph reproduce the admitted gravitational-response family of ordinary CDM while FORCING at least one new nondegenerate prediction?
RELATION COUNT != OBSERVABILITY ORDER.
Couplings can cancel in a visible channel, while a single coupling may be visible; arbitrarily weak couplings can suppress an amplitude signal.
Therefore the stronger object is a typed relation/coupling map together with its production history and response family.

NCDG / DTC FIREWALL:
No current NCDG/DTC result supports the physical claim that dark matter occupies another spacetime, an emergent geometry, or a nonlocal hidden sector. Similar formal language is not evidence of physical transfer.
D. DISCRIMINATORS
No single universal observable separates H1-H4. The labels overlap and each has decoupling limits. Tests must be defined for explicit model pairs.
H1 vs strict gravity-only H2:
A reproducible non-gravitational portal signal would exclude strict gravity-only H2. A null portal search cannot identify H2 because H1 can approach arbitrarily small coupling.
Production-history axis:
Extreme visible-sector sparsity changes which production histories are admissible. Existing CMB/isocurvature and structure data already exclude parts of specific gravity-only production models. This can be cheaper than a new detector, but it is model-specific, not a generic exclusion of H2.
H3 extra-dimensional or brane-separated:
Require signatures forced by the explicit model: KK/radion modes, modified force law, altered gravitational-wave propagation/path/amplitude/polarization, brane cosmology or compact-object effects. Existing GW170817 analyses already constrain some specific braneworld geometries. A decoupling limit can still mimic H1/H2.
H4 emergent-relational geometry:
An explicit map from the model to gravitational observables is required before testing the ontology claim.
The strongest test is one frozen model evaluated jointly against lensing, dynamics, structure growth, CMB and gravitational-wave observables where applicable. A failure constrains that explicit model, not the whole H4 family.

ADMISSION BEFORE IDENTIFIABILITY, but ADMISSION != ONTOLOGY ID.

- relation count alone orders detectability.
