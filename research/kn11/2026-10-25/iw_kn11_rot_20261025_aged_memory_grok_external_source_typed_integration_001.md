event ages u_i in (0,1], the log likelihood ratio between marked Poisson laws

different ages have different LR. The elementary point-process likelihood
therefore independently explains why a count-only readout need not be
sufficient. This check is conditional on the exact intensities above,
**not** a new quantum measurement theorem or evidence of any device.

**B. Arbitrarily weak common gain defeats uniform parameter precision.**
In a declared Gaussian surrogate `X|alpha,t ~ N_d(t*f(alpha),I_d)`,
`alpha in [a,b]`, `t>0` free with no positive lower bound,
`TV(P_{a,t},P_{b,t})=2*Phi(t*||f(a)-f(b)||/2)-1 ->0`
as `t->0`. Thus worst-case absolute-error minimax risk is
`(b-a)/2` (attained by the constant interval midpoint), and a uniform
`1-delta` confidence set necessarily has zero-gain expected Lebesgue
length at least `(1-delta)*(b-a)`. All of this is standard Le Cam/Gaussian
testing, originally developed here for the five-mask surrogate by
