## 1. Theorem R32.1 — exact time-uniform two-reference calibration

For each known reference state i=0,1, let R_{i,k}∈{0,1} represent its kth **recorded** positive outcome, with fixed unknown p_i. Require for every adaptively chosen next reference readout and full past F: P(R_{i,k}=1|F)=p_i, independently of sample allocation, timing and earlier science outcomes. This is a stationary, conditionally Bernoulli assignment **assumption**; it may fail under detector drift or correlated afterpulsing.

Choose strictly positive hyperparameters (a_i,b_i), fixed before the current reference data. Let K_{i,n}=Σ_{k≤n} R_{i,k}. Define

 M_{i,n}(p) = B(K_{i,n}+a_i, n−K_{i,n}+b_i)
             /[B(a_i,b_i) p^{K_{i,n}}(1−p)^{n−K_{i,n}}].    (R32.1)

**Proof:** for each fixed q∈(0,1), L_n(q,p)=q^{K}(1−q)^{n−K}/[p^K(1−p)^{n−K}] is a nonnegative unit-mean martingale under the true p, also with predictably interleaved reference streams. The expression (R32.1) is its mixture over q~Beta(a_i,b_i), hence remains a unit-mean nonnegative martingale. Ville yields

 P_{p_i}(∃n≥0: M_{i,n}(p_i)≥1/α_i) ≤ α_i.             (R32.2)

Set α_0=α_1=.005; at every time n define C_{i,n}={p∈[0,1]:M_{i,n}(p)<200}, including standard boundary conventions at 0 and 1. Each C_{i,n} is an interval (possibly all of [0,1]); its lower and upper bounds are obtained by the two roots of log M=log 200, bracketing p̂=K/n. Combining (R32.2) for both streams gives

 P(∀n0,n1≥0: p0∈C_{0,n0} AND p1∈C_{1,n1}) ≥ .99.  (R32.3)

This is **time-uniform**: one may observe, pause, restart, interleave, reallocate, or stop reference sampling by an arbitrary stopping rule based on the available past. It does NOT allow changing Beta mixing hyperparameters after inspecting the same calibration data without an independent selection or a separately valid higher-level mixture.

**Negative-control theorem:** Repeated fixed-time 99% Clopper–Pearson intervals are *not* a 99% confidence sequence. For any interior Bernoulli p, the law of the iterated logarithm gives fluctuations of K_n−np that exceed every fixed multiple of sqrt(n) infinitely often a.s.; the fixed-time Clopper–Pearson width is O(n^(−1/2)). Thus the true p eventually exits those repeatedly reused fixed-99% intervals with probability 1. One may not simply apply the R31 fixed-size CP proof at an opportunistically selected calibration stopping time.

## 2. Theorem R32.2 — the nuisance pair is shared, not a rectangle of independent c and d

The final detector's mean map is u=c m+d, with ideal pre-detector Z mean m∈[−1,1]. From the actual reference probabilities,

 c=p0−p1,   d=p0+p1−1,
 u(m)=(1+m)p0+(1−m)p1−1.                               (R32.4)

The joint confidence region for (c,d) is a **parallelogram**, the affine image of C_0×C_1; taking its circumscribed independent (c,d) rectangle adds physically impossible nuisance choices. For fixed m, both coefficients 1±m are nonnegative, so the exact extrema of u(m) occur at p0,p1 corners. For the intact pair, with u_j determined by the source-specific Bloch means m_j(Xi), the pointwise expected randomized signed bit is

 F_j(u)=[α_j(1+u)^2+β_j(1−u)^2]/4,
 S(Xi;p0,p1)=Σ_j w_j F_j((1+m_j(Xi))p0+(1−m_j(Xi))p1−1). (R32.5)

As a function of (p0,p1), S is convex quadratic, not an affine function. Thus its maximum over the reference rectangle occurs at one of four corners (sufficient to bound source B); its minimum need not occur at a corner. Let I_S be the **bilinear interpolation of the four corner values** of S at any p0,p1 within the rectangle. Its exact Jensen quadratic gap obeys

 0≤I_S(p0,p1)−S(p0,p1)
  = Σ_j [w_j(α_j+β_j)/4]
      ×[(1+m_j)^2(p0−l0)(u0−p0)
        +(1−m_j)^2(p1−l1)(u1−p1)]
  ≤ H_ref,
 H_ref= [Σ_j w_j(α_j+β_j)] × max{(u0−l0)^2,(u1−l1)^2}/4. (R32.6)

The final inequality uses the fact that the expression in m_j is convex on [−1,1] and attains its maximum at m_j=±1. Therefore S(Xi;p0,p1)≥min_{four corners}S(Xi;corner)−H_ref, for **all** nuisance values and all Xi. This is a certificate, not a numerical QP optimum claim.

For BROKEN A pairs, two distinct pre-detector means m_1,m_2 may each lie arbitrarily in [−1,1]; their two-shot signed probability G_j is bilinear in m_1,m_2 and reaches its minimum at one of their four ±1 corners. With m1=m2=+1, G_j=α_j p0²+β_j(1−p0)²; with m1=m2=−1 the same expression depends on p1; with m1=−m2, G_j=α_j p0p1+β_j(1−p0)(1−p1). The two identical-sign extrema are one-dimensional convex quadratics with explicit clipped stationary points p*=β/(α+β); opposite signs yield bilinear corner minima. This **exact shared-reference reset-floor minimization** replaces the earlier independent-(c,d) rectangle, and preserves the same conservative allowance of arbitrary mid-shot resets.

## 5. Theorem R32.5 — jointly adaptive calibration and science e-processes

In addition to freeze-and-test, one may interleave reference and scientific pairs. Let C_{0,t},C_{1,t} be predictably available CS regions immediately BEFORE the scientific pair t. Either certify a_t>b_t with the same global source analysis and set f_t=f fixed signed rule; or assign evidence factor 1 when the region is not yet separating. With G_A,t(z)=(1−a_t)/(1−b_t)+[a_t/b_t−(1−a_t)/(1−b_t)] f(z) and G_B,t defined by interchanging a_t,b_t, the previous R29–R31 algebra shows conditional mean ≤1 under B and A, respectively, WHEN the true reference parameters remain in the current confidence sets.

For a rigorous global error proof, introduce the *theoretical* first time τ that any CS excludes its true p_i. Consider each factor product stopped just before τ; since all its pre-τ conditional means are ≤1, it is a true nonnegative supermartingale under its respective null. On the event that all CS regions contain the true parameters forever (probability ≥.99), the actual product agrees with the theoretically stopped product. Therefore a threshold 25 for either opposite-null evidence process produces at most .04 conditional scientific false-crossing, plus .01 CS failure for **total ≤.05** per hypothesis, even with adaptive calibration allocation and optional stopping in BOTH streams. If both factors would cross, a predetermined first-crossing policy is used. This is a mathematical extension of source-conditioned e-process validity, not a new optimal all-twelve-alphabet test.
