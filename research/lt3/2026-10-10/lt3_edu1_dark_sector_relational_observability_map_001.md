D. H1–H4 TYPING
H1 — ordinary dark matter in the same spacetime
Carrier is an ordinary matter/field sector coupled to the same metric, with visible non-gravitational portal possibly tiny or zero.
H2 — hidden / gravity-only sector
Carrier may have independent internal interactions but communicates with visible matter only, or dominantly, through gravity.
Important:
in the strict zero-portal limit H1 and H2 can be observationally identical if they generate the same stress-energy / gravitational response and the same initial phase-space law.
Thus the H1/H2 distinction can be semantic rather than observable on the frozen probe set.
H3 — extra-dimensional / brane / multi-metric realization
The hidden carrier may occupy a different brane, bulk sector, or metric sector.
Visible 4D observables arise after projection/transport from the higher-dimensional or multi-metric structure.
H4 — emergent / relational geometry
The fundamental object is not assumed to be an ordinary hidden particle sector in a pre-given common geometry.
The model must supply an explicit constitutive / emergent map from relational or microscopic state to effective geometry and visible observables.
Without that map, H4 is ontology language, not a scientific model.

F. CHEAPEST DISCRIMINATORS
H1 vs strict gravity-only H2:
A reproducible non-gravitational visible-dark interaction is decisive against strict H2:
direct recoil, collider/mediator signal, visible decay/annihilation product, or other source-matched portal.
A null search is not confirmation of H2.
H2 with dark self-interactions:
look for model-specific halo/cluster drag, core structure, dark acoustic features, or CMB/LSS growth signatures.
These test the interaction model, not “hiddenness” itself.
H3:
look for an effect tied to the higher-dimensional / multi-metric structure:
• KK/radion or extra gravitational modes;
• modified short-distance gravity;
• GW propagation/amplitude/polarization anomalies;
• brane/bulk leakage;
• compact-object or cosmological effects not reproducible by the matched H1/H2 baseline.
A sufficiently decoupled H3 can remain observationally degenerate.
H4:
require one frozen constitutive map and test it jointly against:
• lensing;
• dynamical potentials;
• structure growth;
• CMB perturbations;
• where relevant, GW propagation.
The important target is not a one-off anomaly but one shared map that survives several probe classes.
