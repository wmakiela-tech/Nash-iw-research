## 1. Residual

INT8 proved that for a fixed finite number of weak reset measurements, one can exactly separate the observable quotient while the smallest singular value scales as Theta(s), so exact recovery gain scales as Theta(1/s). The remaining cheap escape is to let the number of measurements grow as the maximum per-shot strength tends to zero.

Question: can arbitrarily many individually weak measurements remove the conditioning obstruction while remaining inside the same fixed-sensor reset-flow model?

Answer in the ellipsoidal-storage subclass: NO. If the model admits the existing INT8/Geeky quadratic storage certificate, then the total l2 energy of the entire record generated by any finite protocol whose per-shot strengths are at most epsilon is at most O(epsilon), uniformly over the number and timing of measurements. The corresponding record norm is O(sqrt(epsilon)). This scaling is universally sharp.

## 2. Legal carrier

Let X=R^n, ||e||=1, and free evolution

    x_dot = G x.

At measurement j choose strength

    s_j = 1-u_j,    0 <= s_j < 1,

with reset

    Q_{s_j}=I-s_j e e^T

and normalized scalar record

    b_j = s_j e^T x_j^-.

Let pi be any finite protocol with arbitrary nonnegative waiting times and arbitrary finite number N of measurements.

Assume there exists P>=0 such that

    P e = e,
    G^T P + P G <= 0.

This is exactly the ellipsoidal storage subclass already isolated in GEEKY-S10-INT8-FIXED-SENSOR-AVAILABLE-STORAGE-001 / INT8.

For 0<epsilon<1 define the weak-protocol class by

    0 <= s_j <= epsilon

for every measurement in pi.

## 3. Exact single-measurement dissipation identity

Write a=e^T x for the pre-measurement state and Q_s=I-s e e^T. Because Pe=e and e^T P e=1,

    x^T P x - (Q_s x)^T P(Q_s x)
      = s(2-s) a^2.                                      (3.1)

The recorded scalar satisfies

    b^2 = s^2 a^2
        = [s/(2-s)] [ x^T P x - (Q_s x)^T P(Q_s x) ].    (3.2)

Since f(s)=s/(2-s) is increasing on [0,1),

    b^2 <= [epsilon/(2-epsilon)]
           [ x^T P x - (Q_s x)^T P(Q_s x) ].             (3.3)

This is stronger than the generic storage inequality by an extra weak-strength factor.

## 4. Arbitrary-sample accumulation theorem

**Theorem.** For every finite weak protocol pi with all s_j<=epsilon,

    sum_{j=1}^N b_j^2
      <= [epsilon/(2-epsilon)] x_0^T P x_0.              (4.1)

Equivalently, if C_pi is the full initial-state-to-record operator,

    ||C_pi x_0||_2^2
      <= [epsilon/(2-epsilon)] x_0^T P x_0,              (4.2)

and hence

    ||C_pi||_op
      <= sqrt( [epsilon/(2-epsilon)] lambda_max(P) ).    (4.3)

The bound is independent of N, measurement times, and the individual legal strengths.

**Proof.**
Along each free-flow segment,

    d/dt (x^T P x) = x^T(G^T P+PG)x <= 0.

At every measurement, (3.3) bounds the squared output by epsilon/(2-epsilon) times the drop of P-energy caused by that reset. Summing all measurement drops and inserting the nonpositive free-flow changes telescopes. The total reset dissipation cannot exceed the initial nonnegative storage x_0^T P x_0. This gives (4.1). Equation (4.2) is the operator form and (4.3) follows from x_0^T P x_0 <= lambda_max(P)||x_0||^2. QED.

Thus

    ARBITRARILY_MANY_WEAK_SHOTS
    !=
    UNIFORMLY_STRONG_RECORD_OPERATOR.

In this certified subclass, letting N grow cannot beat the O(sqrt(epsilon)) all-protocol record-norm ceiling.

## 5. Universal sharpness

The factor epsilon/(2-epsilon) cannot be improved uniformly over this class.

Take the scalar model

    n=1, G=0, e=1, P=1,

and perform N zero-wait measurements, all with strength s=epsilon. Then

    x_j^-=(1-s)^{j-1}x_0,
    b_j=s(1-s)^{j-1}x_0,

so exactly

    sum_{j=1}^N b_j^2
      = [s/(2-s)] [1-(1-s)^{2N}] x_0^2.                 (5.1)

As N->infinity through finite protocols,

    sup_N sum b_j^2
      = [s/(2-s)] x_0^2.                                (5.2)

Therefore (4.1) is sharp as an all-finite-protocol supremum. Repetition improves the fixed-N Theta(s) norm scaling only to the best possible Theta(sqrt(s)) scaling; it does not produce an O(1) record operator.

## 6. Exact-recovery conditioning consequence

Suppose a declared nonzero linear functional m^T x_0 is reconstructed exactly from a particular weak protocol by a linear rule w^T b, so

    C_pi^T w = m.

Then necessarily

    ||w||
      >= ||m|| / ||C_pi||
      >= ||m|| sqrt( (2-epsilon) /
                      (epsilon lambda_max(P)) ).          (6.1)

Thus any exact recovery of a fixed nonzero target has gain at least Omega(epsilon^{-1/2}), uniformly over the number and timing of weak measurements.

With deterministic l2-bounded record error ||eta||<=delta, insisting on exact unbiased cancellation incurs worst-case noise term

    delta ||w||
      >= delta ||m||
         sqrt( (2-epsilon) /
               (epsilon lambda_max(P)) ).                 (6.2)

when an exact-recovery w exists.

This does NOT say minimax risk diverges: abstention remains available. It says the exact-inverse route cannot become uniformly well-conditioned by taking more and more weak shots.

## 7. LT5 bounded-gain consequence

For a source-matched terminal target m_pi and declared actuator port a_pi, LT5 gives

    J_kappa(pi)
      = min_{||w||<=kappa||a_pi||}
          [ R||m_pi-C_pi^T w|| + delta||w|| ].

Using (4.3), for every legal w,

    ||m_pi-C_pi^T w||
      >= ||m_pi|| - ||C_pi|| ||w||.

Hence

    J_kappa(pi)
      >= R [ ||m_pi||
             - kappa||a_pi||
               sqrt(epsilon lambda_max(P)/(2-epsilon)) ]_+.    (7.1)

The do-nothing policy gives J_0(pi)=R||m_pi||. Therefore, whenever ||a_pi||<=A,

    0 <= J_0(pi)-J_kappa(pi)
       <= R kappa A
          sqrt(epsilon lambda_max(P)/(2-epsilon)).        (7.2)

This bound is uniform over arbitrary finite sample count and timing.

So for fixed finite kappa and uniformly bounded actuator sensitivity,

    MAXIMUM GUARANTEED IMPROVEMENT OVER DO-NOTHING
      = O(sqrt(epsilon))

throughout the entire weak-protocol class.

A family with nonvanishing target ||m_pi||>=m_0>0 therefore cannot achieve exact cancellation at fixed bounded gain once epsilon is small enough. More quantitatively, exact cancellation requires at least

    kappa||a_pi||
      >= ||m_pi||
         sqrt( (2-epsilon) /
               (epsilon lambda_max(P)) ),                 (7.3)

as a necessary condition.

## 8. Type seams and firewalls

1. The theorem uses the ellipsoidal certificate P. It does NOT prove the same sqrt(epsilon) law for every finite nonquadratic extremal storage V_*.
2. The record is the normalized reset-flow output b_j=s_j e^T x_j^- with l2 geometry. A different noise norm or cost changes the conditioning statement.
3. Arbitrarily many finite shots are allowed, but the model is still classical, fixed-e, deterministic reset-flow. No quantum instrument is inferred.
4. A protocol may alter the physical terminal target itself. Reduction of ||m_pi|| by destructive measurement is TARGET ERASURE, not evidence of actionable information or successful actuator control.
5. If kappa, ||a_pi||, the noise model, or the admissible physical cost is allowed to scale with epsilon, the bounded-gain consequence must be recomputed.
6. HARD AMPLITUDE limits from LT5 turn 2 are a different decision class from the present operator-gain bound.

Firewalls:

    MANY WEAK MEASUREMENTS != O(1) ROBUST INFORMATION AUTOMATICALLY.
    EXACT QUOTIENT SEPARATION != UNIFORM CONDITIONING.
    N->INFINITY != FREE INFORMATION WHEN RESET DISSIPATION IS COUNTED.
    TARGET ERASURE != CONTROL SUCCESS.
    L2 RECORD CEILING != L_INFINITY CEILING.
    QUADRATIC-STORAGE RESULT != GENERAL NONHILBERTIAN V_* RESULT.
