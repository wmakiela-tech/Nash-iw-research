6.4 SOURCE-NATIVE OBJECT
A statistical experiment is a family of observation laws/kernel(s) indexed by the underlying state/parameter.
Blackwell comparison asks whether one experiment can simulate/garble to another and, equivalently under theorem hypotheses, whether it performs at least as well over the relevant class of decision problems.
Therefore:
BLACKWELL INFORMATION ORDER IS DECISION-THEORETIC AND EXPERIMENT-TYPED.
It is not a generic theorem saying:
"richer representation = more information".

- target/decision-relative informativeness;
- exact post-processing/garbling order;
- experiment equivalence;

- "more contexts/data" does not automatically mean more decision value.

7.3 SOURCE-NATIVE OBJECT
The primary object is not a state-space realization.
It is the BEHAVIOR:
the set of trajectories declared possible by the model.
State, input/output split, latent variables and representations are secondary structures used to represent, connect or compute the same declared behavior under hypotheses.
This changes the direction of PRF analysis:
DO NOT BEGIN WITH:
"does this state representation preserve the target?"
BEGIN WITH:
"what trajectory/process behavior is actually declared by the source model, and what variables/interconnections are manifest, latent or legally constrained?"

8. BLACKWELL AND WILLEMS ARE COMPLEMENTARY, NOT ONE HOST
Blackwell:
asks how two observation experiments compare for decisions/inference.
Willems:
asks what trajectories define the system behavior and how representations/interconnections relate to that behavior.
A legal bridge requires at least:
SOURCE / PROCESS BEHAVIOR
->
OBSERVATION / INTERVENTION MAP
->
STATISTICAL EXPERIMENT
->
TARGET / DECISION PROBLEM.
There is no theorem saying every behavioral quotient induces the relevant Blackwell order, or every Blackwell-equivalent experiment identifies one behavior.
Therefore:
BLACKWELL HOST
and
WILLEMS HOST
must stay separately typed.
