7. PASS 2 — THEORY-FIRST SUBTRACTION OF ENDOGENOUS EXPERIMENT GENERATION
7.1 OPTIMAL / ADAPTIVE EXPERIMENT DESIGN AND CONTROL
Mature donor:
optimal experimental design for dynamical systems;
adaptive control;
dual control;
controlled sensing.
Pronzato, Automatica 44 (2008), 303–325:
experiment design and control are explicitly linked.
Input/perturbation choice is part of sequential information acquisition and can be constrained by control objectives.
Older dynamic experiment-design literature already treats constrained inputs/outputs and feedback experiment design.
Consequence:
“the experiment chosen at time t depends on prior observations / current estimate / control objective”
is MATURE.
Generic adaptive experiment generation is not a surviving project theorem candidate.
7.2 CONTROLLED SENSING / POMDP
Controlled-sensing formulations place sensing action selection inside a sequential decision problem.
The action/test policy may depend on the current information state/history and trade observation value against cost.
Consequence:
HISTORY-DEPENDENT TEST SELECTION
is mature.
State/action abstraction, MDP homomorphism and bisimulation provide mature language for when a reduced state/action system preserves control or decision structure.
Therefore:
STATE-DEPENDENT OR HISTORY-DEPENDENT EXPERIMENT CHOICE
!= PROJECT-SPECIFIC RESIDUAL BY ITSELF.
7.3 CAUSAL ABSTRACTION WITH ALLOWED INTERVENTIONS
Beckers–Halpern / approximate causal abstraction explicitly includes allowed intervention sets.
A causal model may contain:
M=(S,F,I)
where I is the set of allowed interventions.
Not all low-level interventions must map to high-level interventions.
The intervention map is part of the abstraction contract.
Current mature extensions include:
- exact transformations;
- uniform transformations;
- approximate causal abstraction;
- soft interventions.
Partially intervenable causal models also study causal identification when only a restricted set of interventions is meaningful/available.
Consequence:
“not every intervention is legal or transportable across levels”
is MATURE.
This is a major prior-art subtraction against generic ENDOGENOUS_EXPERIMENT_GENERATION.
7.4 PROCESS TENSORS / SUPERPROCESSES
Process tensors provide a multi-time process object mapping sequences of interventions/control operations to final states/statistics.
Important distinction:
the underlying process tensor is conceptually separated from the experimenter's chosen control operations.
However mature process-resource theory goes further:
superprocesses describe operationally implementable transformations of multi-time processes;
agent control may be restricted by communication/memory resources;
different operational capacities induce different free transformations and process accessibility.
Conditional control operations and correlated instruments/testers are already within the mature multi-time framework.
Consequence:
MULTI-TIME CONTROL WITH RESTRICTED OPERATIONAL CAPACITY
is substantially mature.
The generic quantum version of “available experiments depend on control resources” is therefore not an unoccupied theory space.
7.5 QUANTUM REFERENCE FRAMES / OPERATIONAL ACCESS
Operational QRF work defines frames using operational objects such as covariant POVMs/instruments and relative observables/effects.
Frame changes are defined on operational equivalence classes under stated assumptions.
Recent 2026 QRF literature makes the accessibility question explicit:
different frameworks disagree in part because they assume different operational capacities, including accessibility of global charge.
Recent generalizations also move beyond fixed tensor-factor frames toward covariant instruments and resource-theoretic conditions.
Consequence:
FRAME / RESOURCE STATE CONTROLS OPERATIONAL ACCESS
is already a live mature research theme.
Again:
this cannot be claimed as a generic NASH theoretical surplus.

9. WHAT, IF ANYTHING, SURVIVES?
Only a much narrower candidate seam:
SOURCE-NATIVE INTERVENTION-FAMILY TRANSPORT ACROSS CHANGING REPRESENTATIONS.
Typed form:
Given a source-native process/behavior object B,
a context/frame/representation c,
an admissible intervention correspondence I_c(B),
an observation map O_c,
and a target tau,
when two representations c and c' are declared equivalent for the scientific claim,

- process/behavior;
- admissible intervention family;
- observation semantics;
such that the induced experiment/decision structure is preserved at the declared target?
This is NOT claimed to be new.
It may still be absorbed by:

- process/superprocess theory;
- constrained MDP morphisms;
- QRF operational frame transformations;
- categorical/open-system behavior theory.
The residual is only worth pursuing in a SOURCE-SPECIFIC case where one of those hosts provably fails to carry the intervention family without changing the scientific semantics.
