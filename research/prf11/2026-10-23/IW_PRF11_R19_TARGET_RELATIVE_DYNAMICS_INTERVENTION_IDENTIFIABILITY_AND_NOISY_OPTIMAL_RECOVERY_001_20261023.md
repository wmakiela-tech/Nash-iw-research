## 2. Source-typed and QUANTIFIER-CORRECT visible-subspace statement

Let n>=1, V⊊R^n be a FIXED A-invariant linear subspace, and let
   C(A,V)={A' in R^(n×n) : (A'-A)|V=0},
without imposing extra physical sign, locality, sparsity, stability, or parameter constraints. For a candidate NEW preparation w and target ell≠0 consider the scalar FUTURE TRAJECTORY
   f_A'(t;w,ell)=ell^T exp(A' t) w.

**Proposition R19.1 (mature host specialization).**
For this UNRESTRICTED completion class, f_A'(·;w,ell) is uniquely determined as a function of time on every [0,eps] over A'∈C(A,V) if and only if w∈V.

Proof:
If w∈V, invariance A'V=AV⊂V and equality of restrictions imply (A')^k w=A^k w for every nonnegative integer k, hence exp(A't)w=exp(At)w for all t≥0 and every ell.

If w∉V, choose a linear functional phi annihilating V but with phi(w)=1, and a vector z with ell^T z=1. For any nonzero scalar gamma let A_gamma=A+gamma z phi^T. Then A_gamma agrees with A on V, so belongs to C(A,V). But
   d/dt [ell^T exp(A_gamma t)w -ell^T exp(At)w] at t=0
      = gamma ell^T z phi(w)=gamma≠0.
Analyticity of finite-dimensional matrix exponentials implies the target trajectories differ for all sufficiently small t>0. Thus no agreement on any complete nontrivial initial time interval can be guaranteed. QED.

**Exact nonmap/qualifier:** The negative direction is about an unrestricted completion class, not ALL physically restricted source models. An observable at a PARTICULAR FIXED future instant can accidentally coincide across several models even if whole-trajectory identification fails. Conversely the positive direction is robust for w∈V and every target ell.

**Critical controllability distinction:** In the original Ulusarslan experiment class, B's image is by construction IN V. Every future input through the SAME known B, started in V, therefore remains inside V and is predictable from A|V; there is no contradiction with Theorem 3.7. An intervention that inserts a component outside V necessarily CHANGES the allowed initial conditions, actuator, or source intervention class. This change requires its own legality check before any empirical claim.

## 3. A sharp STABLE two-dimensional counterexample

Fix a=1 and a parameter beta∈[beta_min,beta_max], 0<beta_min<beta_max. Define
   A_beta=diag(-a,-beta), x0=e1=(1,0)^T, B=0.

For every beta, the whole, exactly measured baseline state trajectory is
   x_beta(t)=exp(-at)e1.
Thus even INFINITE exact temporal sampling of the baseline, with all state coordinates observed, cannot distinguish beta. The visible space is V=span{e1}. It is A_beta-invariant, and the restricted generator is identifiable, but A_beta on the complement is not.

For any known fixed initial w∈V, all future ell-targets remain predictable. In contrast, consider a HYPOTHETIC legally authorized *new* intervention/pulse after the baseline at time tau, setting the new state to x(tau^-)+d e2, d>0. It is a NEW actuator/different initial-state class, not the original B=0 experiment.

For the terminal scalar target ell=e2 at delay Delta=T-tau>0, the change in target caused by the pulse is
   g_beta=d exp(-beta Delta).
Every beta∈[beta_min,beta_max] matches the original experiment yet gives a different g_beta.

From the baseline ALONE, the set of possible terminal target values is exactly
   [d exp(-beta_max Delta),d exp(-beta_min Delta)].
Under worst-case absolute error, the exact minimax prediction is its midpoint and the irreducible risk is
   E0=(d/2)[exp(-beta_min Delta)-exp(-beta_max Delta)].      (3.1)

This is ordinary Chebyshev/optimal recovery in a one-dimensional feasible target interval; it does not imply any source-valid neutron, PBH, quantum or DTC experiment.

## 4. A new noisy probe and EXACT scalar target minimax risk

Set d=1 for clarity, beta∈[beta_min,beta_max], 0<h≤Delta. AFTER the hypothetical pulse, the experiment additionally records the same e2 target at delay h:
   Y=exp(-beta h)+epsilon,  |epsilon|≤eta,  eta≥0.       (4.1)
The unknown error is deterministic bounded adversarial, NOT Gaussian. There is ONE new probe. We seek a prediction of g_beta=exp(-beta Delta) from Y with worst-case ABSOLUTE loss over beta and epsilon. No extra observations, no external source calibration or independent new preparations are allowed.

Let
   x=exp(-beta h),  x_min=exp(-beta_max h),
   x_max=exp(-beta_min h),  kappa=Delta/h≥1.
Then x∈[x_min,x_max], Y=x+epsilon and g=x^kappa.
Conditionally on a received Y=y, the entire feasible x interval is
   I_y=[max(x_min,y-eta), min(x_max,y+eta)]
provided the interval is nonempty. Since g=x^kappa is continuous increasing, the feasible target interval is [left_y^kappa,right_y^kappa]. Its Chebyshev midpoint
   ghat(y)=[left_y^kappa+right_y^kappa]/2             (4.2)
has the minimal worst-case conditional error equal to half the interval's diameter.

**Proposition R19.2 — EXACT global minimax risk, all measurable estimators:**
   E*(h,eta)
   = (1/2)[exp(-beta_min Delta)
      -max{exp(-beta_max h),exp(-beta_min h)-2eta}^(Delta/h)].          (4.3)

Proof: two x values share at least one Y with noise |epsilon|≤eta iff |x-x'|≤2eta. Thus the global minimax risk is half the largest |x^kappa-x'^kappa| among x,x'∈[x_min,x_max] and |x-x'|≤2eta. For kappa≥1 the derivative kappa*x^(kappa-1) is nondecreasing, so for a fixed permitted separation the maximal image separation is achieved at the HIGHEST interval endpoint x_max and x'=max(x_min,x_max-2eta). The midpoint (4.2) attains that half-diameter for every feasible y. This proves the identity without assuming a linear estimator or Gaussian noise. QED.

Exact controls:
- If eta=0 and h>0, beta is determined uniquely from one post-pulse reading and E*=0.
- If 2eta≥x_max-x_min, the measurement cannot exclude both extreme beta values for some Y, so E*=E0.
- At h=Delta, direct noisy measurement of the exact target gives E*=min(eta,E0).
- If eta>0 is FIXED and h↓0, x_max-x_min ~ (beta_max-beta_min)h<2eta eventually, so E*→E0. An arbitrarily EARLY probe is exactly useful without noise but entirely uninformative at the minimax level with fixed bounded noise.
- The inverse beta=-(1/h)log x has local derivative d beta/dx=-1/(h x); near h=0, instability blows up ~1/h. This is about BOUNDED-NOISE conditioning, not a structural impossibility in the noiseless model.

## 5. Independent numerical controls and exact falsifiers

   beta=2: g=0.20189651799465538;
